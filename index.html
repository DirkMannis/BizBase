The fleet, plainly

We put vehicles and operations on the ground in Nebraska. National autonomous networks match riders to the nearest available car. We do not control network dispatch, and we do not promise that an agency trip will arrive in a BizBase vehicle.

Vehicles here

Purpose-built robotaxis and capable EVs based in Nebraska — charged, cleaned, maintained, and kept in service so capacity exists locally.

Network rides

Trips run on national autonomous networks. The network sends the nearest available vehicle. That is how the service scales.

Local operations

Depot, charging, supervision, and incident response sit here — so Nebraska is not waiting on a fleet that only exists somewhere else.

Who the service is for

Public rides

When a network is live in the area, anyone can hail an autonomous trip the same way they would in cities where the service already operates.

Business and campus transport

Employee shuttles, shift changes, campus loops, and visitor movement — capacity on the ground for trips employers already cover with vans and personal cars.

Agency and civic trips

Agencies use the public network for needs they already have. Standing coverage is an operations conversation later — not exclusive fleet priority.

What Nebraska can help establish

Nebraska already authorizes driverless-capable vehicles and on-demand driverless networks. The work now is making a local fleet physically possible — then usable, then trusted.

01
